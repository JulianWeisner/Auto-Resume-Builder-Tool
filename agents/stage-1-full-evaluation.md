# Stage 1 — Full evaluation (1.2–1.4)

Part of the [agent guide](../AGENTS.md). **Load when:** the user asks for the full evaluation, and before Stage 2 plans a résumé (`plan.md` uses the 1.3 and 1.4 definitions). **Also read:** the [fit check rules](stage-1-evaluate.md), which hold the job-description key, the scores and the score key, and [plain language](writing/plain-language.md).

**1.2 Asks-and-stories table.** Build it in this order:
1. **Score key**, printed above the table: the score key from the [fit check rules](stage-1-evaluate.md#preparation-always), with its colors.
2. **Table.** Use exactly **Their wording (job description) | What they're really asking | Your best story (plain English) | Score | Your records**.
   - Column 1: shorthand placeholders for the asks the row covers, for example `{Do-1'} {Want-3'}` (or tags plus short verbatim quotes when typing the key by hand). Full-quote placeholders (`{Do-1}`) belong only in the key; `assemble` rejects them elsewhere.
   - Column 2: the plain-English ask. Merge redundant bullets; this is not a one-to-one rewrite. Label each ask **(must)** for core responsibilities and stated requirements, **(important)** for secondary ones, **(nice-to-have)** for anything the job description marks as preferred, ideal or nice to have, or **(context, not stated)** for company or domain context the description implies, such as the customers served. Context rows also carry inferred hiring expectations: secondary expectations the description implies but does not state. Never score them as mandatory.
   - Column 3: at most two plain sentences covering the situation, what the user did and the result. Keep essential qualifiers such as shared ownership, recalled metrics or unlaunched status. Merge context rows that share a story; use at most two context rows.
   - Column 4: the score with its color: your judgment of how well the row's best story meets the ask, as a reference for the user. It may differ from the private per-tag scores.
   - Column 5: record IDs.
   - Keep rows that score 0 in the table, and say what is missing. A gap in the record is not proof that the user lacks the experience.
3. **Not mapped.** List every tag not used in the table or storyboard, with a reason. `assemble` prints the unused tags.

**1.3** **Storyboard (résumé outline):** Sketch the résumé structure this evidence supports, in the same strict reverse chronology as the résumé ([style guide](../templates/resume/style-guide.md#structure-and-punctuation)): newest first by end date, including client engagements within a practice and roles within an employer; where end dates match, put the later start first. List each employer or practice, the selected functional title and dates, and under each the stories to feature. Use one line per story: name it in a few words (the user knows the stories), then state which job requirements it demonstrates as placeholders, for example `★ Returns redesign → {Do-1'} {Want-3'}`, which `assemble` expands to the job description's own words with their tags. Mark lead stories ★. Do not retell stories, cite records or repeat scores; the table above holds the evidence. Keep each title within its supported window and place each story under the role whose dates contain it. Sequential functional roles may share one heading when pooling their stories reads better; join them with an arrow and span their combined window, for example **[Earlier Title] → [Later Title] · [earlier start] – [later end]**, so no title borrows another's start. Decide grouping fresh for each résumé across the whole employer's story (and the user's [presentation preferences](../career/preferences.md#résumé-presentation)): any sequential labels may be combined or shown as separate headings, and no pairing from an earlier résumé is a default. Never pool or slash-join the alternatives of one either-or label set (for example Chief of Staff and Director of Operations). Keep numbers out of storyboard lines; the table holds them with their qualifiers. Apply the [distinct-value test](../AGENTS.md#distinct-value-test): two stories pointing at the same requirements need a distinct reason to stay. End with **Not covered:** the important requirements no selected story covers, one line each as `{tag'} → where to address it` (summary, cover letter or interview). This list, with the table's low scores, is the only home for experience gaps. Cover experience only: omit Education, Skills/Tools and the personal signature, which Stage 2 adds from the style guide. This is a plan, not finished résumé wording.

**1.4** **Review before build**: what the user should confirm before the résumé is built, limited to what the table cannot show: presentation traps (titles and their date windows, consulting framing, chronology), documentation limits that change wording (for example recalled figures), and conflicts in the record. At most five bullets, one sentence each; name the storyboard line a bullet affects, for example "Returns redesign → under Operations Lead (2021), not Operations Analyst." Do not restate experience gaps. Assess chronology and title ambiguity without treating concurrent consulting clients as separate short-term jobs or functional labels as formal promotions.
