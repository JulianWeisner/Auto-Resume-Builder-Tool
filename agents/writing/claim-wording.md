# Claim wording

Part of the [agent guide](../../AGENTS.md). **Load when:** writing or revising résumé bullets, summaries, cover letters or application answers. **Also read:** [plain language](plain-language.md).

## What makes a strong bullet

A strong bullet lets a stranger picture **what was done, by whom, at what scale and what changed**. The usual shape is a problem, the user's action or experiment, and a result or learning. Not every bullet needs every part; never invent a missing outcome.

| Weak | Why | Stronger (when the record supports it) |
|---|---|---|
| Responsible for onboarding process | A duty, not an action or result | Redesigned the onboarding checklist for new support agents; time to first solo shift fell from 3 weeks to 2 |
| Spearheaded cross-functional synergies | Inflated words, no content | Ran a weekly review with engineering and sales that cut open customer escalations by about a third |
| Experience in data analysis | Vague; no ownership | Built the monthly churn report the leadership team used to set retention targets |

These examples show translation choices, not facts or required wording.

## Writing guidance

- Explain an unfamiliar organization's work once in plain language, with relevant, qualified scale.
- Select concrete evidence of the behaviors the job asks for. Prefer a clear problem, personal action and result over generic responsibility lists.
- Translate internal names into the work performed, the people served and what the work enabled. For features, explain what users provide, what the feature produces and how they use the result, with only the detail needed. Introduce an internal name only with a short functional explanation. Keep technical detail when it shows relevant expertise; avoid both unexplained company jargon and wording so vague it could describe any candidate.
- Show inexpensive prototyping, decisions under uncertainty and progression from experiments to delivery when supported. Working at a startup does not by itself establish those behaviors. Customer analytics is not a substitute for collaboration with researchers; exploring AI is not shipping AI.
- Preserve ownership, collaborators, delivery state, metric denominator, population and period during compression. A pilot moving from two to three active weeks per month is not 50% recurring month-over-month growth. Do not turn participation into sole ownership or company scale into personal revenue.
- Keep bullets tight and distinct. Use meaningful numbers selectively; do not repeat an outcome across bullets or the summary.
- Before delivery, check every claim against the current records, then read as someone unfamiliar with the company: can they picture what was done, its scale and why it mattered? Keep record IDs and review notes out of employer-facing copy. Past résumés and positive reactions to drafts are not new evidence.
- Follow each role's `presentation_note` for how it appears to employers (for example, client engagements shown under one consulting practice). A presentation choice never changes dates, entities or IDs in the record.
- Résumé metrics generally need no calculation or footnote. Keep the remembered basis and uncertainty in the record; do not keep asking for missing calculations when qualified firsthand evidence already supports useful wording. Never turn a recollection into independently verified causality.
- Fit the emphasis to the role family: for product roles, product decisions, customer outcomes and technical collaboration; for operations roles, process, cost and reliability; and so on. Include scientific or technical detail only where it adds relevant evidence.
- Where before and after values are known, a simple derived percentage may help; keep its formula, unit and population in the record. Do not relabel frequency as retention or invent a composite measure.
- Identify internal software by its users and purpose ("an internal authoring tool for a 12-person content team"). Lead influence stories with the initiative and its adoption; lead strategy stories with what was developed and presented, keeping shared ownership.

## Final wording checks

- In summaries, state supported ownership, capabilities and actions. Replace vague "experience in" with what the candidate owns, defines, develops or delivers. Match tense to chronology; stronger wording must not expand ownership or advance delivery status.
- Prefer plain descriptions ("internal testing") over jargon ("dogfooding") when the jargon adds nothing.
- Lead with assertive, supported ownership and action. Prefer a conservative supported number to repeated qualifiers ("doubled", not "at least doubled"). Do not turn a supported floor into "more than" that floor.
- Explain complex work through concrete relationships: who does what, the constraints and who benefits.
- Show influence through persuasion across reporting lines and the resulting adoption when supported. Phrases such as "outside my reporting line" or "without formal authority" are useful when they establish the requested competency.
- End with a supported outcome where useful: a measured improvement, launch or adoption. Use evidence instead of superlatives such as "industry-leading."
- Before presenting a draft, check that a stranger understands the work, action and outcome; each selected story passes the [distinct-value test](../../AGENTS.md#distinct-value-test); strong relevant metrics survive; compression preserves ownership and metric meaning; and removable phrases are cut.
- Preserve the user's direct voice: owned, scoped, prototyped, redesigned, shipped, prioritized, won buy-in. Correct grammar and tighten sentences without softening supported claims.
- Make accountability concrete through supported decisions, tradeoffs and results relevant to the target role; distinguish team output from personal contribution.
- Records often date events by the user's employment ("after I left", "after the restructuring"). In employer-facing copy, state the work's status and timing without tying it to the user's joining or leaving: "The app was built; the launch was later canceled", not "…canceled after my departure".
- Keep causal attribution separate from tone. A wording preference does not establish stronger causality.
