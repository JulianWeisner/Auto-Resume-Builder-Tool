# Cover-letter style guide

[Templates](../README.md) · [Default cover-letter template](README.md)

Start from the [editable template](cover-letter.html); the [rendered PDF](cover-letter.pdf) is the visual reference. Story choice and wording depend on each role: never reuse another application's examples or opening by default. Facts follow [claim wording](../../agents/writing/claim-wording.md) and the career records; generated letters are not evidence.

## Write the letter

1. **Open with why this work matters to the user.** Start with a real decision, experience or motivation from the records or from the Motivation section of [career/preferences.md](../../career/preferences.md#motivation). Then connect it to something specific the employer does or wants to build. Never invent enthusiasm or a personal connection. A generic opening ("I am excited to apply…") is filler; a specific reason replaces it. If nothing is recorded, ask the user one question about why this role appeals to them before drafting.
2. **Develop two or three relevant examples.** For each, show who had the problem, what the user did and what changed. Use one meaningful number or sign of adoption where the records support it. Match the scale of the solution to the problem. For consulting or business-to-business roles, name the client or workflow when it can be shared and say how the work was used.
3. **Connect the examples to the employer's work.** Name a concrete contribution the user wants to make. Check the posting's own description of the role before describing it.
4. **Close simply.** A brief thank-you and the user's name on its own line, then the optional personal signature.

## Plain-language pass

Apply [plain language](../../agents/writing/plain-language.md). In particular:

- Use short, active sentences and familiar words. Give each sentence one main job.
- Prefer concrete actions such as built, tested, measured and handed off. Explain technical terms only where the reader needs them.
- Keep the actor, scope, metric and result. Keep pilots, internal tools, deployed products and partner outcomes distinct: a pilot result stays attached to the pilot users and period.
- Keep the opening in the user's voice. Replace generic praise with a specific reason for wanting the work.
- Keep sensitive details (for example health information) and internal application notes out of employer-facing copy unless the user chooses to include them.

## Layout

| Element | Default |
|---|---|
| Page | US Letter portrait, 0.75-inch margins, black Arial, left-aligned body. Aim for one page. |
| Name and contact | Name in 22 pt bold. Contact on the next line in 10 pt with 13 pt leading: location, email, phone and website from [career/contact.json](../../career/contact.json), separated by vertical bars. Matches the résumé header. |
| Subject | Optional. 10.5 pt bold above the greeting: the posted role title, plus the requisition number when the employer gives one. Leave it out when it adds nothing. |
| Greeting and body | 10.5 pt with 15 pt leading and 12 pt between paragraphs. More space below the contact line than between paragraphs. |
| Sign-off | Thank-you and typed name on separate left-aligned lines, kept together with the signature. |
| Personal signature | Optional, off by default. The same wording and style as the [résumé signature](../resume/style-guide.md#personal-signature-optional): centered Arial 9.5 pt, below a centered 0.5 pt gray rule extending 0.25 inch past the text on each side. Delete the block when unused. |

## Before delivering

Replace every bracketed prompt, confirm contact values and any role title or requisition number, and verify each metric against the career records. Render to PDF at US Letter, 100% scale, without browser headers or footers. Open the PDF and check the page count, line wraps, subject and signature placement, and that the text is selectable. If the letter spills onto a second page, edit for length first.
